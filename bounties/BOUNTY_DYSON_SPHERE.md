🎯 Objective
Implement a fully simulated Dyson Sphere stellar engineering framework within the SS13 Unreal Engine 5 codebase. The Dyson Sphere is not merely a background decoration — it is an active, interactable megastructure that powers the station, generates resources, and serves as both a narrative driver and a catastrophic failure point.

This expansion must deliver a player-visible, physically simulated Dyson swarm of solar collectors orbiting the system's star, integrated with the station's power grid, material economy, and round progression. The swarm must be constructible, expandable, and destructible. It must obey realistic orbital mechanics at a simplified but convincing level of abstraction. Every department on the station must have a stake in its operation — engineers maintain it, scientists research upgrades, cargo supplies materials, security defends it from solar-saboteur antags, and the AI commands its defense grid.

The Dyson Sphere is the ultimate engineering project. It must feel like one.

🧠 Required Systems (Detailed Breakdown)
1. Orbital Shell Simulation
A simplified Keplerian orbital model for collector satellites arranged in concentric shells. Each shell represents a construction tier (I through V). Satellites produce power proportional to shell density and stellar luminosity. Shells can be damaged, repaired, and upgraded independently.

2. Power Beaming & Grid Integration
Energy collected by the swarm is beamed to the station via microwave rectenna arrays. Power output must integrate with the existing powernet simulation (cable graph, SMES units). Station-side receivers require precise alignment — misalignment causes power loss or catastrophic beam drift (read: holes in the station).

3. Material Economy & Construction Pipeline
New cargo-crate types: solar collector panels, orbital deployment drones, rectenna components. Engineering must coordinate with Cargo to order materials. Science must research shell-efficiency upgrades. Construction requires EVA-capable crew or remote-operated drones to deploy satellites in orbit.

4. Stellar Dynamics & Catastrophic Failure Modes
The star has a luminosity curve that can be influenced by player actions (stellar injection, dark matter experiments). Shell collapse events: a cascade failure in one shell can trigger Kessler Syndrome across the entire swarm, destroying power output and creating a debris field hazard. Emergency protocols include shell jettison and controlled de-orbit.

5. Antagonist Integration
New antag objectives: "Sabotage the Dyson Swarm," "Redirect power beam into the station," "Induce stellar flare." Traitors can purchase orbital sabotage kits. Nuke ops can target the rectenna ground station. Cultists can attempt to "awaken the star" as a living entity.

6. AI & Borg Integration
Station AI gains a "Stellar Management" module — the ability to monitor shell status, adjust power routing, and trigger emergency protocols. Engineering borgs receive orbital deployment capability. The AI's law set must interact with Dyson-related decisions (e.g., "protect the station" vs "protect the swarm").

7. UI & Visualization
A dedicated Stellar Engineering console (UMG-based) showing real-time orbital shell status, power output graphs, and alert indicators. Exterior camera feeds showing the swarm from station viewports. Orbital map overlay for EVA deployment planning.

8. Round Progression & Endgame
Completing Shell V triggers a station-wide "Post-Scarcity Protocol" — unlimited power for the remainder of the round, but also escalates all antag threats to maximum. The Dyson Sphere becomes a victory condition and a target simultaneously.
