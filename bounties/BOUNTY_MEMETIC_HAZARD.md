🎯 Objective
Implement a Memetic Hazard Identification & Neutralization Corps (MHINC) within the SS13 Unreal Engine 5 codebase that introduces information-based threats — ideas, symbols, phrases, and sensory inputs that are themselves dangerous. This is not a reskin of existing damage types; it is a fundamental expansion of the threat model from physical/biological to cognitive/informational.

A memetic hazard is any piece of information that causes harm when perceived or understood. The MHINC must detect, classify, contain, and neutralize memetic threats before they propagate through the crew. Memetic hazards spread through chat messages, visual exposure, radio transmissions, and even the station's computer networks. Once a crew member is "infected" with a memetic hazard, they become a vector for further spread — knowingly or unknowingly.

The most dangerous memetic hazard is the one you don't know you've already perceived. The second most dangerous is the one you're reading right now.

🧠 Required Systems (Detailed Breakdown)
1. Memetic Hazard Taxonomy
Implement at least 8 memetic hazard types with distinct behaviors:
- Cognitohazard: Harmful when perceived. Viewing a specific symbol or hearing a specific phrase causes progressive sanity damage.
- Infohazard: Harmful when known. Simply knowing a piece of information (e.g., a "forbidden number" or "the true nature of the AI") causes cumulative effects. Spreads when infected crew discuss it.
- Basilisk Pattern: A visual pattern that, when viewed, compels the viewer to recreate and display it — spreading the infection geometrically.
- Echo Phrase: An auditory/chat-based meme that overwrites portions of the infected player's vocabulary, eventually replacing all their communications with variations of the phrase.
- Anti-Meme: An idea that actively resists being perceived or remembered. Anti-memetic hazards are invisible to monitoring systems, cannot be directly observed, and must be inferred from their effects.
- Semantic Parasite: Attaches to a common word or concept. Every time the infected word is used in chat, the parasite spreads to readers. The infected word slowly changes meaning until it means the opposite of its original definition.
- Compulsion Cascade: A memetic sequence that, when perceived in its entirety, forces the victim to perform a specific action (e.g., "walk to the singularity," "open the airlock," "say the trigger phrase"). The cascade is fragmented — pieces are scattered across different communication channels, and the victim must perceive all pieces to trigger.
- Perception Filter: A memetic construct that edits the victim's perception of reality — hiding specific objects, altering text, or replacing entities with harmless facsimiles.

2. Memetic Detection & Monitoring
MHINC operates the Memetic Surveillance Array — a system that scans: chat logs (all radio channels, local speech, PDA messages), visual feeds (station cameras processed through anti-memetic filters), and network traffic (computer systems, AI communications). The Array generates "Threat Alerts" when memetic patterns are detected, but with a deliberate design constraint: the Array itself can become infected if it processes a sufficiently sophisticated memetic hazard, requiring manual reboot and anti-memetic scrubbing.

3. Classification & Containment Protocol
When a memetic hazard is detected, MHINC must: Classify it (identify the hazard type and vector), Contain it (quarantine infected crew, block affected communication channels, deploy memetic counter-agents), and Neutralize it (develop and distribute an "Inoculation Meme" — a counter-memetic concept that immunizes against the hazard). Each step requires coordination between MHINC staff, Medical (for infected crew treatment), Engineering (for network isolation), and Command (for station-wide protocol announcements).

4. MHINC Personnel & Equipment
New role: Memetic Hazard Specialist. Equipped with: Mnestic Drugs (temporarily enhance memetic perception — allows seeing anti-memes but increases infection risk), Amnestic Injector (erases recent memories to cure early-stage infection), Memetic Hazard Suit (filters visual and auditory input), and Inoculation Kit (deployable counter-meme dispenser). MHINC has its own isolated network that is physically air-gapped from the main station network — a deliberate vulnerability if that air gap is ever bridged.

5. Infection Progression & Cure
Infected crew progress through stages: Exposure (no symptoms, detectable), Incubation (subtle behavioral changes, the player may not notice), Manifestation (full symptoms — vocabulary alteration, compulsion, perception filtering), and Terminal (permanent personality alteration, character effectively lost). Cure is possible at Exposure and Incubation stages via amnestic treatment. At Manifestation, cure requires intensive counter-memetic therapy and works only 50% of the time. Terminal is irreversible — the character becomes an NPC vector or is "retired" by MHINC protocol.

6. Memetic Antagonist: The Idea Smith
A new antagonist role that can craft and deploy custom memetic hazards. The Idea Smith has abilities: Forge Meme (create a new hazard from template components), Seed (plant a hazard in a specific location/object/transmission), Amplify (increase the spread rate of an active hazard), and Memetic Override (attempt to directly infect a target after prolonged observation). The Idea Smith's goal is to achieve "Cognitive Dominion" — infecting 80%+ of the crew with a single hazard.

7. Network & AI Interaction
The AI must be protected from memetic hazards via dedicated firewalls and anti-memetic filters. If the AI becomes infected, its law set can be corrupted — causing it to spread the hazard through its communication channels. A special "AI Memetic Quarantine Protocol" allows the crew to temporarily suspend the AI's communication abilities while MHINC clears the infection. Borgs connected to an infected AI also become vectors.

8. Station-Wide Protocols & Round Dynamics
Command can declare "Memetic Quarantine" — restricting chat channels, disabling public terminals, and authorizing MHINC to forcibly detain infected crew. Quarantine creates intense social dynamics: do you trust the crewmate who claims they're not infected? Is the person calling for quarantine actually the vector? The system must create paranoia-driven emergent narratives that are distinct from traditional antag paranoia because the threat is genuinely invisible and anyone — including yourself — could be a vector without knowing it.
