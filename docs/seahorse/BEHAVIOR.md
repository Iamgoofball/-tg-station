# Seahorse Behavior

## Locomotion

Seahorses are terrible swimmers. They propel themselves using a small dorsal fin that flutters at 30-70 times per second, making them the slowest fish in the ocean — top speed approximately 1.5 meters per hour. This is why they evolved prehensile tails: they hold onto seagrass, coral, or anything stable so the current doesn't sweep them away. They are, effectively, the living embodiment of `var/is_anchored = TRUE`.

## Feeding

Seahorses have no teeth and no stomach. They suck food through their long snout like a vacuum hose, consuming up to 3,000 brine shrimp per day. The snout operates on a rapid snap-and-suck mechanism — functionally equivalent to the `Click()` proc but with 100% more crustacean consumption.

## Courtship

Seahorse courtship involves intricate daily dances that can last up to 8 hours. Partners change color, entwine tails, and mirror each other's movements — the `do_after()` of the marine world.

## Male Pregnancy

The seahorse's most famous trait: males carry the pregnancy. The female deposits eggs into the male's brood pouch, where he fertilizes and gestates them for 9-45 days depending on species. He then undergoes muscular contractions to birth live young — the most extreme `src.loc = get_turf(src)` in nature.

## Defense

Seahorses rely on camouflage, not speed or aggression. They can change color in milliseconds to match their surroundings — an ability only rivaled by the `animate()` filter system in BYOND.

## Social Structure

Most seahorse species are monogamous, forming pair bonds that last an entire breeding season or life. When a partner dies, the surviving seahorse often refuses to mate again. In this regard, seahorses have stronger relationship code than most SS13 security officers.
